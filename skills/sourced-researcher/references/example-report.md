**Question:** "When did Python 3.12 reach its first stable release, and what is one headline feature?"

---

Python 3.12.0 was released on 2 October 2023 [1]. A headline change is the new,
more readable f-string parsing (PEP 701), which lifts several long-standing
restrictions on what can appear inside an f-string [2]. The release also focused
on error messages and per-interpreter sub-interpreter groundwork [1].

Key points
- 3.12.0 stable landed on 2 October 2023 [1]
- PEP 701 reworked f-string parsing (nesting, quotes, multiline) [2]
- Improvements to error messages shipped in the same release [1]

Sources
[1] Python 3.12.0 release page — python.org — https://www.python.org/downloads/release/python-3120/
[2] PEP 701 – Syntactic formalization of f-strings — https://peps.python.org/pep-0701/

Confidence & gaps
Release date and PEP 701 are confirmed by primary python.org sources. "One
headline feature" is a judgement call — PEP 701 is the most cited, but 3.12 had
several notable changes; this report did not rank them exhaustively.

---

## Why this passes

- Every factual sentence ends with a citation.
- Both sources are **primary** (python.org / peps.python.org), not blogs.
- The "Confidence & gaps" line is honest about what is a judgement call.
- No claim rests on a single tertiary source.
- Nothing is asserted that wasn't on a page that was actually opened.
